## Why I built this

Every other interactive piece on this site — Narrate, Airgap, Maze
Runner — is entirely self-contained. Open the page, everything runs in
your browser, no external dependency, nothing that can go down but the
site itself. This one is different on purpose: it's the first project
here with a real hardware dependency, because "Hardware & IoT
Integration" is a service I actually offer, and the honest way to prove
that is to connect something real, not simulate it.

## What it is

Three pieces:

- **This page** — client-only, same as everything else here. It has no
  backend of its own; it talks directly to the bridge below from your
  browser.
- **A local bridge** (`matter-bridge/` in this repo) — a small Node.js
  process using [matter.js](https://github.com/project-chip/matter.js)
  that speaks Matter directly to a real smart plug on my home network:
  commission once, then read/write its OnOff cluster on every
  connection.
- **A Cloudflare Tunnel** — gives that local process a stable public
  HTTPS address without exposing my home network directly, and without
  a third-party relay service in between.

## Why it's opt-in, not 24/7

This isn't a limitation dressed up as a feature — it's the actual
engineering answer to "how do you let the public internet flip a switch
on a device in your house without that being a bad idea." The bridge
only runs when I start it. When it's not running, this page says so
honestly rather than faking a live demo. When it is running, a rate
limiter and a 15-second auto-off timer mean no single visitor — or
script — can do anything worse than turn a lamp on for fifteen seconds.

## What made it hard

Filled in once the bridge has actually run against real hardware —
worth writing from what happened, not what I expected to happen.

## Honest limitations

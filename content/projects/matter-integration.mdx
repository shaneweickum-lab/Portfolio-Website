## Why I built this

Every other interactive project on this site runs entirely in your browser, with nothing outside it that could go down. This one is different on purpose — it's the first project here that connects to a real, physical device, because helping businesses connect their physical equipment to their software is something I actually offer, and the honest way to prove that works is to connect something real instead of faking it.

## What it is

Three simple pieces working together: this page (which talks directly to the next piece from your browser, nothing hidden), a small local program on my own home network that speaks directly to a real smart plug, and a secure tunnel that lets this page reach that program safely from anywhere, without exposing my home network to the internet.

## Why it's only on sometimes, not all the time

This isn't a shortcut dressed up as a feature — it's the genuinely responsible answer to "how do you let visitors flip a switch on a real device in someone's house without that being a bad idea." The connection only runs when I turn it on. When it's off, this page says so plainly instead of faking a live demo. When it is on, safety limits mean no visitor can do anything more than briefly turn a lamp on or off.

## Honest limitations

This section gets written last, after everything has actually been tested against the real device — not written ahead of time based on what I expect to happen. This page stays hidden from the main site until that testing is complete.

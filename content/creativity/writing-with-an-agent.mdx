The instinct when you build AI systems for a living is to point them at
everything, including your own fiction. I've mostly resisted that, for one
reason: the thing that makes a world or a voice worth reading is exactly the
kind of specificity a language model will average away if you let it.

Where AI tooling actually earns a place in the process:

- **Consistency checks.** Feeding a model my faction notes and asking it to
  flag contradictions is faster and more honest than my own memory.
- **Interrogation, not generation.** Asking "what would the Tidewrit Concord
  actually do here, given what it wants" produces better material than
  asking it to write the scene.
- **Timeline and continuity bookkeeping.** The unglamorous work — who knew
  what, and when — is exactly the kind of task I'm happy to hand off.

Where it doesn't belong: the actual sentences. Voice is the part I'm not
willing to outsource, which is also, not coincidentally, the part that made
me want to write in the first place.

## The problem

Anyone who's tried building a novel or a fictional world with an AI across
many sessions runs into the same failure mode eventually: facts drift. A
character's age changes. A minor detail invented for one scene quietly
contradicts something established three chapters earlier. The AI starts
inventing plot-relevant details on the fly because it's easier than
asking, and by book two nobody — including you — is entirely sure what's
actually canon anymore.

## What it does

This skill is a *process*, not a *style*. It doesn't impose a genre, a
tone, or a set of tropes — it imposes discipline: a locked, growing set of
canon documents; a hard rule that new facts get flagged instead of
invented silently; and prose-quality checks calibrated to *your* stated
preferences, not a house style baked into the skill.

The first time it runs on a new project, it doesn't jump straight into
writing — it interviews you first (genre, format, how strict you want
canon control to be, your prose preferences, your length targets) and
builds a governance document from your actual answers. That document
becomes the standing rulebook every session checks against afterward.

From there:

- **New facts get flagged, not invented.** If a scene needs a detail your
  canon doesn't cover, it stops and asks before writing it in.
- **You decide what becomes canon.** Nothing is locked in until you say
  so explicitly.
- **Minor characters and places earn real backstory.** The moment a name
  recurs, it stops being a throwaway detail and gets folded into your
  world with actual history.
- **Length targets are a compass, not a cage.** Chapters and books can run
  shorter or longer than your stated range when the story genuinely calls
  for it — a short book gets a check for underdeveloped threads, never
  padding.
- **Series-level planning stays honest.** Open questions stay open rather
  than getting papered over with an overconfident roadmap you never
  actually agreed to.

## Choosing your canon strictness

- **Strict** — nothing enters canon without your explicit sign-off. Best
  if you've been burned by AI-introduced continuity errors before, or
  you're building something with a lot of interlocking rules.
- **Moderate** — small, inconsequential details can be invented freely;
  anything structural still gets flagged. The middle ground for most
  projects.
- **Loose** — creative latitude by default, you course-correct as needed.
  Best for drafting-fast, revising-later workflows.

## What it won't do

It won't invent your world for you, write in a voice you didn't ask for,
or impose genre conventions you didn't choose. It won't quietly decide
your canon-strictness preference or your style rules on your behalf. Its
entire job is enforcing whatever discipline *you* choose to work under —
consistently, honestly, and without ever pretending a gap in your canon
isn't there.

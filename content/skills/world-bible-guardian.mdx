## The problem

The longer a world runs — across books, drafts, and rewrites — the harder
it gets to hold every established fact in your head while you're heads-down
writing a new scene. A character's eye color changes between chapters. A
travel time that took three days in book one takes one day in book three.
A magic system rule quietly gets broken because the scene needed it to.
None of these are big mistakes on their own; they're small, and they
accumulate, and by the time an editor or a reader catches one, there are
usually several.

## What it does

Given your world bible (characters, timeline, geography, magic/tech rules,
terminology) and a manuscript passage, this skill cross-checks the new
prose against established canon and reports back three kinds of findings:
what's consistent, what actually contradicts something already
established, and what's new worldbuilding that isn't in the bible yet —
which isn't necessarily wrong, just undocumented, and worth a deliberate
decision either way.

It's built to flag and cite, not to silently rewrite. A contradiction
might be a mistake, or it might be an intentional retcon the bible hasn't
caught up to yet — that's the author's call, not the skill's.
